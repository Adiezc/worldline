# The world on 1 October 1962

**The superpowers.** Seventeen years after Hiroshima, the United States and the Soviet Union face each other across a divided world. America holds a large lead in deliverable nuclear weapons: some 200 ICBMs, Polaris submarines and a huge bomber force against a few dozen Soviet ICBMs. Kennedy, stung by the Bay of Pigs (April 1961) and the Vienna summit, has expanded conventional forces and runs Operation Mongoose, a CIA sabotage campaign against Castro. Congressional midterm elections are due on 6 November and Republicans attack the administration over Cuba.

**Cuba.** Castro has declared himself a Marxist-Leninist. The OAS excluded Cuba in January and the US has imposed a near-total embargo since February. Since July, Soviet ships have been arriving in unusual numbers. U-2 flights in August and September photographed SA-2 surface-to-air missile sites, MiG-21 fighters, coastal-defence cruise missiles and Komar missile boats. Moscow insists all of it is defensive. On 4 and 13 September Kennedy warned that "the gravest issues would arise" if offensive weapons appeared in Cuba, and Congress is completing a joint resolution authorising force if necessary. U-2 overflights of western Cuba have been restricted since early September for fear of the SAMs.

**Berlin and Germany.** The Berlin Wall has stood since 13 August 1961. Khrushchev still demands a German peace treaty that would end Western rights in West Berlin, and threatens to sign a separate treaty with Ulbricht. Adenauer, now 86, is building a special partnership with de Gaulle, who toured West Germany in triumph in September. (On this map, West Berlin lies inside the East German province of Brandenburg.)

**Europe.** Western Europe is booming. The six-member Common Market is working, and Britain, Denmark, Ireland and Norway have applied to join. Britain's negotiations in Brussels are stuck over Commonwealth trade and agriculture, and de Gaulle is sceptical. France has just ended the Algerian war (Évian accords, March; independence, 5 July). The settler terrorist OAS nearly killed de Gaulle at Petit-Clamart on 22 August; he has called a referendum for 28 October on direct presidential elections. Albania has sided with China and stopped taking part in the Warsaw Pact and COMECON. Tito's Yugoslavia is non-aligned.

**The communist schism.** The Sino-Soviet split is now open. Soviet experts left China in 1960 and the two parties trade polemics. China is recovering from the Great Leap Forward famine, which killed tens of millions. Along the Himalayan border, India's "forward policy" has placed posts in territory China claims. There have been clashes in Ladakh and, since 8 September, Chinese troops have pressed on Indian positions below the Thag La ridge in the North-East Frontier Agency. Nehru does not believe China will start a major war.

**Southeast Asia.** The Geneva agreement of 23 July 1962 neutralised Laos under a coalition led by Souvanna Phouma, but the Pathet Lao and North Vietnamese troops remain and the Ho Chi Minh Trail runs through the country. In South Vietnam the Viet Cong insurgency is growing. Diem's government relies on strategic hamlets and some 9,000 American advisers, while helicopter units fly ARVN troops into combat. In August the Netherlands agreed to hand West New Guinea to Indonesia. The UN Temporary Executive Authority (UNTEA) takes over today, 1 October, and Indonesia gets the territory on 1 May 1963. This scenario keeps it Dutch until then. Britain plans to merge Malaya, Singapore, North Borneo and Sarawak into Malaysia by August 1963. Sukarno and the Philippines (which claims Sabah) object.

**Middle East.** On 26 September army officers under Abdullah al-Sallal overthrew Imam al-Badr, who had succeeded his father only a week earlier, and proclaimed the Yemen Arab Republic. The Imam escaped to the northern mountains, where Saudi Arabia and Jordan back royalist tribes. Nasser has promised military support, and the first Egyptian troops are on their way. Syria broke away from the United Arab Republic in September 1961, although Egypt keeps the name. Iraq's Qasim claims Kuwait and is fighting a Kurdish revolt led by Mustafa Barzani. Israel faces hostile neighbours along the 1949 armistice lines. Jordan holds the West Bank and Egypt administers Gaza.

**Africa.** Decolonisation is sweeping the continent. Seventeen states became independent in 1960. Since then Sierra Leone, Tanganyika, Rwanda, Burundi and Algeria have followed, and Uganda follows on 9 October. Britain still rules Kenya, Zanzibar, the High Commission Territories, Gambia and Aden. The Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland is crumbling: Britain has accepted that Nyasaland may secede. In the Congo, Katanga's secession under Tshombe continues despite UN forces and the U Thant reunification plan of August. Portugal is fighting a rebellion in Angola that began in 1961 and refuses to decolonise. South Africa has left the Commonwealth, banned the ANC and holds South West Africa. Nelson Mandela was arrested in August.

**Latin America.** In Argentina, the military overthrew Frondizi in March and its factions fought in the streets in September. Peru has been under a junta since July. Brazil's Goulart rules under a parliamentary system imposed to limit him. Venezuela's Betancourt faces leftist guerrillas and put down two military revolts in May and June. The Dominican Republic heads for its first free election since Trujillo's assassination.

**The space race and technology.** Glenn orbited the Earth in February; in August the Soviets flew two Vostok capsules at once. On 12 September Kennedy pledged to reach the Moon. Both sides have tested nuclear weapons in the atmosphere throughout 1962.
