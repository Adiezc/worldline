# The world on 1 January 2026

**Russia-Ukraine.** The war is nearly four years old. Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine, including Crimea, most of Luhansk and much of Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts. Its slow, costly offensive grinds forward around Pokrovsk, Huliaipole and toward Kramatorsk and Sloviansk. Ukraine strikes Russian refineries, ports and the shadow tanker fleet with long-range drones; Russia pounds Ukraine's power grid. From November 2025 the Trump administration pushed a peace plan, first drafted with Russian input and then revised with Kyiv and the Europeans. The sticking points are Russia's demand for the rest of Donetsk, limits on Ukraine's army, NATO non-membership, and what security guarantees the US and a European "coalition of the willing" (led by the UK and France) would give. The EU agreed to fund Ukraine for 2026-27 with a large joint loan after failing to agree on using frozen Russian assets. A corruption scandal around the state nuclear company cost Zelensky his chief of staff. No ceasefire is in force.

**Middle East.** A US-brokered ceasefire in Gaza has held loosely since 10 October 2025. All living hostages were released; Israel still controls roughly half the Strip behind the "yellow line". UN Security Council Resolution 2803 endorsed a Board of Peace and an International Stabilization Force, but no troop contributor will fight Hamas, which refuses to disarm. Strikes and killings continue on both sides. Israel also keeps striking Hezbollah in Lebanon, where the government is trying to disarm the group, and holds positions in southern Syria. In June 2025 Israel and then the US struck Iran's nuclear and missile sites in a 12-day war. Iran's enrichment programme is badly damaged but its stockpile of highly enriched uranium is unaccounted for. In September the E3 triggered the snapback of UN sanctions, which Russia and China call illegal. Iran's currency is collapsing, and it faces water shortages and power cuts. Israel goes to elections by October 2026.

**Syria** is ruled by President Ahmed al-Sharaa, who visited the White House and joined the anti-ISIS coalition. Most US, EU and UK sanctions have been lifted, and the Caesar Act was repealed in December 2025. Integration of the Kurdish-led SDF has stalled, and sectarian massacres of Alawites and Druze in 2025 left deep distrust.

**Africa.** In Sudan the RSF captured El Fasher in October 2025 amid mass killings and now holds almost all of Darfur, while fighting moves into Kordofan. The army government sits in Port Sudan and Khartoum. Famine is widespread, and the UAE is accused of arming the RSF. In eastern DR Congo the M23, backed by Rwanda, still holds Goma and Bukavu despite a US-brokered DRC-Rwanda peace agreement (signed in Washington in June and December 2025) and a Doha framework with M23; fighting continues. The juntas of Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger have left ECOWAS, formed the Alliance of Sahel States and expelled French forces. They rely on Russian partners while jihadists blockade Bamako's fuel supply. In late 2025 coups took power in Madagascar and Guinea-Bissau, and a coup attempt in Benin failed. Ethiopia-Eritrea tension is high over Ethiopia's push for sea access.

**Americas.** The US has massed warships in the Caribbean, struck dozens of alleged drug boats since September 2025, designated the Cartel de los Soles a terrorist organisation and is pressing Maduro to go. Venezuela is under blockade-like pressure, and a war or a regime collapse is possible. Relations with Colombia's Petro are hostile. Argentina's Milei won the October midterms with US financial backing. Chile elected José Antonio Kast, Bolivia elected Rodrigo Paz, and Honduras elected Nasry Asfura, all on the right. Brazil votes in October 2026.

**Asia.** China and Japan are in their worst spat in years after PM Sanae Takaichi said a Taiwan contingency could threaten Japan's survival. Beijing responded with travel warnings, a seafood ban and military pressure. The PLA keeps up large exercises around Taiwan. The US and China agreed a one-year trade truce in Kuala Lumpur and Busan (October-November 2025): tariff cuts, a pause on rare-earth export controls and soybean purchases. India and Pakistan fought a four-day air and missile conflict in May 2025 after the Pahalgam attack; the ceasefire holds, but there is no dialogue. Thailand and Cambodia fought border battles in July and December 2025 despite a peace accord signed with Trump in October. Myanmar's junta is holding phased elections from 28 December 2025, widely seen as a sham, while losing ground to resistance armies. Pakistan has clashed with the Afghan Taliban across their border.

**Caucasus.** Armenia and Azerbaijan initialled a peace agreement at the White House in August 2025, including a US-run transit route through southern Armenia (the "Trump Route", TRIPP). The final treaty awaits Armenian constitutional change.

**Trade and economy.** US tariffs are at their highest level since the 1930s: a 10% baseline, high "reciprocal" rates and sectoral duties on steel, cars and other goods. Deals with the EU, UK, Japan, Korea, Vietnam and others reduced rates in exchange for investment pledges. The Supreme Court has heard the challenge to the emergency (IEEPA) tariffs but not yet ruled. Global growth is about 3%, carried by a huge AI investment boom that has driven up US stock valuations amid bubble fears. Inflation has mostly eased but remains sticky in the US and UK; the Fed has cut rates to 3.5-3.75%. Oil is cheap (Brent around $60) as OPEC+ unwinds cuts. Gold is at record highs and China's exports are at a record surplus. Debt burdens in France, the UK and the US worry markets.
